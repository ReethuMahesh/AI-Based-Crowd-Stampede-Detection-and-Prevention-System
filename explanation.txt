src/detector.py
What is this file?
Think of detector.py as the eyes of your system.
It loads the YOLOv8 model and looks at each video frame. It finds every person in that frame, draws a green box around them, and returns how many people it found and where they are.
Simple analogy: Like a security guard who watches a screen and circles every person he sees with a marker.
How it connects:
main.py
  → calls detector.py
      → uses YOLOv8 model
          → returns people count + their positions
              → positions go to zones.py and heatmap.py later


src/density.pyWhat is this file?
Think of density.py as the crowd counter and judge.It takes the people count from detector.py and does two things:

Counts how many people are in the frame
Judges if that count is Low, Medium, or High density
Simple analogy: Like a mall security system that counts how many people entered and decides if the mall is empty, busy, or dangerously overcrowded.How it connects:
detector.py → returns people count
    → density.py receives that count
        → returns "Low" / "Medium" / "High"
            → risk.py uses this label later


src/optical_flow.py
What is this file?
Think of optical_flow.py as the motion detector of your system.
It compares two consecutive video frames and calculates how much things moved between them. If people are walking slowly — low motion. If people are running or panicking — high motion.
Simple analogy: Like comparing two photographs taken 1 second apart and measuring how much everything shifted.
How it connects:
main.py sends two frames (previous + current)
    → optical_flow.py compares them
        → returns motion magnitude (a number)
            → returns "Normal" or "Fast"
                → risk.py uses this label


src/risk.py
What is this file?
Think of risk.py as the brain and decision maker of your system.
It takes two things:

Density label from density.py → "Low" / "Medium" / "High"
Motion label from optical_flow.py → "Normal" / "Fast"

And combines them into one final decision → 🟢 Safe / 🟡 Medium Risk / 🔴 High Risk
Simple analogy: Like a doctor who looks at two test results (blood pressure + heart rate) and gives a final verdict on your health.
How it connects:
density.py  → gives "Low" / "Medium" / "High"
optical_flow.py → gives "Normal" / "Fast"
    → both go into risk.py
        → risk.py outputs "Safe" / "Medium Risk" / "High Risk"
            → alert.py uses this to trigger alerts



src/zones.pyWhat is this file?
Think of zones.py as the area inspector of your system.It divides the video frame into 4 equal zones (like cutting a pizza into 4 slices) and checks which zone has too many people in it.Simple analogy: Like a stadium divided into 4 sections — security checks which section is overcrowded and needs attention.

How the zones look:
┌─────────────┬─────────────┐
│             │             │
│  Zone 1     │  Zone 2     │
│  Top-Left   │  Top-Right  │
│             │             │
├─────────────┼─────────────┤
│             │             │
│  Zone 3     │  Zone 4     │
│  Bot-Left   │  Bot-Right  │
│             │             │
└─────────────┴─────────────┘
How it connects:
detector.py → returns center points of every person
    → zones.py receives those center points
        → checks which zone each person falls into
            → counts people per zone
                → flags crowded zones
                    → main.py displays it



What is this file?
Think of heatmap.py as the heat vision of your system.It creates a colourful overlay on the video that shows where people are concentrated. Areas with many people glow red/yellow, empty areas stay blue/green.Simple analogy: Like a weather map — red areas are "hot" (crowded), blue areas are "cold" (empty).How the heatmap looks:
┌─────────────────────────────┐
│  blue   blue   RED   blue   │  ← RED = many people here
│  blue   YELLOW yellow blue  │  ← YELLOW = medium crowd
│  blue   blue   blue  blue   │  ← blue = empty
└─────────────────────────────┘How it connects:
detector.py → returns center points of every person
    → heatmap.py receives those center points
        → builds a heat layer (brighter where more people)
            → blends it over the video frame
                → main.py displays the result



Step 9 — src/alert.py
What is this file?
Think of alert.py as the alarm system of your system.
It watches the risk level every frame. If risk stays High for too many consecutive frames — it triggers a big visual alert on screen saying "HIGH RISK ALERT!"
Simple analogy: Like a fire alarm — it doesn't go off the moment it detects one smoke particle. It waits to confirm the danger is real, then rings loudly.
Why we wait for consecutive frames:
Frame 1 → High Risk  ← don't alert yet
Frame 2 → High Risk  ← don't alert yet
Frame 3 → Medium     ← reset counter
Frame 4 → High Risk  ← start again
...
Frame 10 consecutive High Risk → 🚨 ALERT!
This prevents false alarms from a single bad frame.
How it connects:
risk.py → returns "Safe" / "Medium Risk" / "High Risk"
    → alert.py watches that label every frame
        → counts consecutive High Risk frames
            → if count > threshold → triggers alert
                → draws alert on frame
                    → plays sound (optional)


main.pyWhat is this file?
This is the heart of the entire project.It ties every single module together and runs the full pipeline frame by frame. Think of it as the director — it tells every module when to work, collects their results, and puts everything on screen.How it works frame by frame:
Open video
    → read one frame
        → detect people        (detector.py)
        → count + density      (density.py)
        → measure motion       (optical_flow.py)
        → calculate risk       (risk.py)
        → analyze zones        (zones.py)
        → update heatmap       (heatmap.py)
        → check alert          (alert.py)
        → display everything   (cv2.imshow)
        → save frame to output video
    → read next frame
        → repeat until video ends
